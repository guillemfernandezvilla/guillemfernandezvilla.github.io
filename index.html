Current Research

My dissertation research examines how perceptions of immigrants are shaped by vicarious intergroup contact and sociolinguistic cues. I investigate how accent and place of origin influence interpersonal inferences and engagement with immigrant communities.

I am particularly interested in how emotional acculturation processes affect immigrant wellbeing and how cultural psychology frameworks can inform our understanding of intercultural relationships and integration experiences.

About

I am a PhD candidate at the University of California, Santa Barbara in the Department of Psychological and Brain Sciences, working with Dr. Heejung Kim in the Cultural Psychology Lab. My work focuses on immigrant perceptions, acculturation processes, and intergroup relations using both experimental and large-scale quantitative methodologies.

Before beginning my doctoral studies, I earned my M.Sc. in Cultural Psychology from the University of Amsterdam and worked as a Junior Researcher at the Netherlands Institute for the Study of Crime and Law Enforcement, where I examined police legitimacy and procedural justice for immigrant communities across Europe.

Selected Projects

  • Intercultural Close Relationships
    How do cultural differences shape the dynamics of close relationships, including romantic partnerships and friendships? This work examines challenges such as communication barriers and value misalignments, as well as adaptive processes like cultural learning and the development of shared relational practices.
  • Vicarious Intergroup Contact and Immigrant Perceptions
    This project investigates how observing interactions between immigrants and majority group members influences interpersonal inferences about immigrants. The research examines the mechanisms through which vicarious contact shapes attitudes and behavioral intentions toward immigrant communities.
  • Accent Bias and Place of Origin
    How do linguistic cues interact with information about immigrants' place of origin to shape impressions and evaluations? This work contributes to understanding the multifaceted nature of immigrant stigma and discrimination in contemporary society.
  • Police Legitimacy in Europe
    Using large-scale cross-national data, this research identifies how immigrant status and experiences of ethnic discrimination influence perceptions of police legitimacy across European contexts, with implications for policing policy and community relations.
